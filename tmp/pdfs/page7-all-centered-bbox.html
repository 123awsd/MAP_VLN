Implementation details. Our runner executes precom- piled CPTGs and uses local OWLv2 for online veri- fication, while semantic recovery can invoke Qwen3.8- Flash 1 with temperature 0 and thinking disabled. All planning parameters are fixed across scenes and tasks: (v xy , v z , ω ψ ) = (1.0 m/s, 0.5 m/s, 1.0 rad/s), (ℓ 0 , τ 0 ) = (1 m, 1 s), (β d , β Q , β z , β r , β b ) = (1, 1, 0.10, 0.15, 0.15), and (η L , η T , η V ) = (1, 0.25, 2). LogicFly CT is its native task- completion time excluding preparation and post-hoc evalua- tion, whereas baseline CT is measured from process start to terminal state; all CT values are averaged only over successful episodes. Every process has a 240 s wall-clock limit, and timeouts and algorithm or infrastructure failures remain in the full denominator. B. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods in Simulation We compare LogicFly with HM3D adaptations of OneMap [37], MapGPT [27], SpatialGPT [31], and Spatial- Nav [28]. All methods run on the same 854-episode clean schedule. OneMap retains its native ordered-category pro- jection, which omits relations, conditional branches, verified absence, recovery, and return constraints; its SR therefore should not be interpreted as strict full-instruction semantic success. MapGPT, SpatialGPT, and SpatialNav use the frozen common strict evaluator, while LogicFly uses its frozen native strict complete-task evaluator. As shown in Table I, LogicFly consistently outperforms the compared methods across target count and floor scope. On single-target tasks, it reaches 97.78% SR, 87.28% SPL, and 8.19 s CT on single-floor episodes, and 94.74% SR, 86.03% SPL, and 8.99 s CT on cross-floor episodes. On the harder multi-target tasks, it maintains a 96.70% SR on both floor scopes, with SPL values of 82.49% and 81.45% and CT values of 10.33 s and 14.70 s for single-floor and cross-floor episodes, respectively. No method receives private target geometry; timeouts and algorithm failures remain unsuccessful terminal outcomes. C. Evaluation in Real-World Environments Experimental platform. We conduct real-world exper- iments using a custom-built quadrotor equipped with an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (16 GB), a PX4 flight controller, a Livox Mid-360 LiDAR, and an Intel RealSense D435 depth camera. The D435 provides synchronized 640 × 480 RGB and depth observations for online verification and 3D projection. FAST-LIO [38] provides onboard localization, while LiDAR- based occupancy mapping supports real-time trajectory op- timization. The UAV is constrained to a maximum linear velocity of 1.0 m/s, linear acceleration of 1.0 m/s 2 , and angular velocity of 1.0 rad/s. As illustrated in Fig. 1, LogicFly completes a three-target instruction end to end with a physical UAV in a real multi- room environment. The 3D reconstruction provides only a bird’s-eye visualization of the physical layout and executed trajectory; all navigation, observation, and verification are performed in the real environment.
